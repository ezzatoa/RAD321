Background and model assumptions

Read before changing the controls
Theory primer

Distortion is a misrepresentation of object size or shape. It occurs when the anatomical part is angled or displaced away from the perpendicular central ray. Foreshortening occurs when the anatomical part is tilted relative to the perpendicular beam. Elongation occurs when the part is displaced laterally into the divergent peripheral beam.

Alignment goal: anatomy centered over perpendicular CR and anatomy plane ∥ IR. Moving an object laterally away from the central ray exposes it to divergent oblique rays that physically elongate its recorded shadow.

Distortion is not simply “blur.” A sharp but distorted image may still fail the diagnostic criterion because anatomy is represented incorrectly.

Model boundary: Results are directionally and mathematically useful for learning. They are not calibrated exposure charts, dosimetry, or manufacturer specifications.
