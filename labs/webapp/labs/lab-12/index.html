Background and model assumptions

Read before changing the controls
Theory primer

Digital radiographic detectors and CT scanners feature a wide, linear dynamic range capturing thousands of discrete attenuation levels (e.g., −1000 HU to +1000 HU). Because human visual perception can distinguish only about 30–100 shades of gray simultaneously on a monitor (typically 256 display levels in 8-bit output), windowing (grayscale mapping / LUT transformation) is necessary:

  • Window Width (WW): Controls the range of pixel values mapped across 0 (pure black) to 255 (pure white). It determines displayed image contrast. A narrow window expands subtle tissue differences into steep contrast gradients (high contrast), but clips outlying anatomy. A wide window accommodates a wide dynamic range (broad latitude), displaying bone and soft tissue simultaneously at lower contrast.
  • Window Level (WL): Sets the central midpoint pixel value of the display window. It controls overall image brightness and directs visual attention to specific tissue types (e.g., +40 HU for mediastinum/soft tissue, +400 HU for bone, −500 HU for aerated lung).
  • Clipping & Saturation: Values below Tlow = WL − WW/2 clip to black; values above Thigh = WL + WW/2 saturate to white.
  • Exposure Latitude & Exposure Creep: Digital systems automatically rescale raw detector signals to provide acceptable monitor brightness. However, an underexposed image exhibits elevated quantum mottle due to photon starvation, while an overexposed image looks pristine but imparts unnecessary radiation dose. Monitoring the Exposure Index (EI) and Deviation Index (DI) is vital to prevent dose creep.
Active Display Passband: Tlow = WL − WW/2  to  Thigh = WL + WW/2 (HU).
Look-Up Table (LUT) Contrast Gain: G = 255 / WW (gray levels per HU). Slope of the grayscale mapping ramp.
IEC Deviation Index (DI): DI = 10 · log₁₀(EI / EIT). Target range is −1.0 to +1.0 DI; DI > +1.0 indicates dose creep; DI < −1.0 indicates quantum noise risk.
Subtle Lesion CNR: CNR = ΔGray / σeffective, where σeffective = √(σdisplay² + σquantum² · 100/EI).

Model boundary: Results are directionally and mathematically accurate for teaching digital contrast, LUT mapping, and ALARA principles. They are not calibrated diagnostic workstations or equipment dosimetry logs.
