Theory primer

Image noise is unwanted statistical fluctuation that obscures subtle anatomical details and low-contrast lesions. In medical radiography, total noise consists of two primary independent sources:

  • Quantum Noise (Quantum Mottle, σq): Arises from the discrete, random Poisson arrival of X-ray photons at the detector. When fewer photons are detected (low mAs or low DQE), statistical fluctuations are relatively large. The standard deviation of quantum noise is σq = S / √Ndetected.
  • Intrinsic Electronic Noise (σint): A fixed background noise floor generated by detector readout electronics, thermal fluctuations, and digitization, independent of radiation exposure.
  • Total Combined Noise (σtot): Because these noise sources are statistically independent, they combine in quadrature: σtot = √(σq2 + σint2).
Object Signal (S, in arbitrary units `a.u.`): Mean transmitted X-ray intensity / pixel value in a region of interest.
Detected Quanta (N): N ∝ mAs × DQE.
Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR): SNR = S⁄σtot = (Mean Signal S) / (Total Noise σtot).
Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR): CNR = |Starget − Sbackground|⁄σtot.
Rose Criterion: A visual psychophysics threshold establishing that an SNR ≥ 5 (or CNR ≥ 3) is necessary for human observers to reliably detect a subtle lesion.
Detective Quantum Efficiency (DQE): DQE = SNRout2 / SNRin2. Measures how effectively a detector preserves input SNR per unit radiation dose.

Why does increasing Object Signal increase Noise SD? In Poisson processes, the absolute photon variance increases as the signal increases (σ ∝ √N). On a fixed-scale monitor, larger absolute fluctuations appear as larger pixel-to-pixel variations. However, increasing total photon fluence (mAs) increases detected photons much faster than noise grows, steadily improving the relative SNR (SNR ∝ √mAs).

Model boundary: Values are expressed in arbitrary units (a.u.) to teach normalized physical relationships and statistical scaling laws without requiring equipment-specific dosimetric calibration.
