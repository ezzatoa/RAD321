Background and model assumptions

Read before changing the controls
Theory primer

Fluoroscopy produces a sequence of images using continuous or pulsed X-rays. In an image-intensifier system, the entrance phosphor converts X-rays to light, the photocathode converts light to electrons, electrostatic lenses focus them, and the output phosphor converts electrons back to light for the camera/display.

Brightness Gain: Minification Gain × Flux Gain = (d_in / d_out)² × Flux Gain. Magnification Mode: higher electrostatic lens voltage moves the electron crossover closer to the photocathode, reducing active FOV and increasing patient dose rate via ABC compensation. ALARA Protection: minimize time, maximize distance (1/d²), and utilize protective lead shielding.

Modern systems may use flat-panel detectors, but the principles remain: real-time feedback, automatic exposure control, field-size selection, dose awareness, and correct shielding.

Model boundary: Results are directionally and mathematically useful for learning. They are not calibrated exposure charts, dosimetry, or manufacturer specifications.
