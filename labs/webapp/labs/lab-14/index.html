Background and model assumptions

Theory primer

The IMAGE Process Stage E (Evaluate) provides the final, rigorous quality assurance check before a radiograph is transmitted to PACS for radiologist interpretation. Clinical image evaluation follows 5 structured pillars:

  • 1. Anatomical Coverage & Collimation: Ensure the entire Volume of Interest (VOI) is included (distal ⅓ femur to proximal ⅓ tib/fib) without clipping critical landmarks, with clear 4-sided collimation margins to limit scatter.
  • 2. Positioning & Alignment: For a True AP Knee, the femoral epicondyles must be parallel to the IR, the femorotibial joint space fully open, the patella centered over the distal femur, and exactly ⅓ of the proximal fibular head superimposed behind the lateral tibial condyle.
  • 3. Exposure & Noise Physics: Verify detector exposure using the standard Exposure Index (Target EI = 200, DI = 0.0). Avoid photon starvation (DI < −1.0, severe quantum mottle) and unnecessary dose creep (DI > +1.0).
  • 4. Identification & Lead Markers: Verify patient demographics (2 unique identifiers) and the presence of a physical radiopaque lead side marker ('R' or 'L') placed in the primary beam outside the VOI.
  • 5. Artifacts & Motion: Eliminate preventable external radiopaque items (zippers, snaps, jewelry) and assess for patient motion blur or hardware detector line defects.
ALARA Repeat Rule: A repeat exposure is clinically justified only when a defect obliterates diagnostic efficacy and cannot be rectified without re-irradiating the patient.
Missing Marker Policy: A missing lead marker on an otherwise diagnostic image MUST NOT be repeated. Departmental policy dictates electronic annotation and dual-technologist verification.
Critique Weighting: Score = 0.30·SAnat + 0.25·SPos + 0.25·SExp + 0.10·SMark + 0.10·SArt.

Model boundary: Results are directionally and mathematically accurate for teaching clinical image critique and ALARA gating. They are not calibrated equipment exposure charts.
