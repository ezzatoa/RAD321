Background and model assumptions

Read before changing the controls
Theory primer

Digital radiography uses three distinct physical capture technologies:

  • Computed Radiography (CR): Uses a reusable photostimulable phosphor (PSP, BaFBr:Eu²⁺) cassette plate. X-ray absorption traps electrons in metastable energy states. A red helium-neon laser scans the plate, stimulating blue-violet photostimulated luminescence (PSL) collected by a photomultiplier tube (PMT). Turbid crystal light scatter reduces high-frequency MTF.
  • Indirect Conversion DR: Uses a needle-structured Cesium Iodide (CsI:Tl) scintillator that converts X-rays into visible light, which is subsequently converted into charge by an amorphous silicon (a-Si) photodiode array and stored in TFT capacitor elements. Structured needles channel light, mitigating but not eliminating lateral light spread.
  • Direct Conversion DR: Uses an amorphous Selenium (a-Se) photoconductor under high-voltage electrostatic bias (+5 kV). Incident X-rays generate electron-hole pairs collected vertically with zero lateral light scatter, providing the highest inherent MTF and spatial sharpness.
Matrix Pixel Size: Pixel Size (mm) = FOV (mm)⁄Matrix Size = (FOV (cm) × 10)⁄Matrix Size.
Effective Sampling Pitch: peff = max(pDEL, pmatrix). The limiting aperture defines the sampling interval.
Sampling Frequency: fs = 1⁄peff (samples/mm).
Nyquist Frequency Limit (fN): fN = fs⁄2 = 1⁄(2 × peff) in line pairs per mm (lp/mm).
Shannon-Nyquist Theorem & Aliasing: To faithfully record an anatomical structure with spatial frequency f, the receptor must sample at ≥ 2 points per cycle (fs ≥ 2f ⇒ f ≤ fN). Spatial frequencies exceeding fN fold back as false, lower-frequency Moiré / Aliasing patterns (falias = |fs − f|).
DEL Fill Factor: Active Sensing Area⁄DEL Area × 100%. Smaller DELs have lower fill factors due to fixed TFT electronics.
Limiting Spatial Resolution (10% MTF): R10% ≈ fN × MTFmaterial. Minimum resolvable object width Δx = 1⁄(2 × R10%).

Matrix-Limited vs. Detector-Limited: When Pixel Size > DEL Pitch, image resolution is bottlenecked by the matrix (increasing matrix improves detail). When DEL Pitch > Pixel Size, image detail is constrained by physical detector elements, and larger matrices provide empty magnification without new diagnostic signal.

Model boundary: Results are mathematically and physical-conceptually accurate for teaching digital sampling, Nyquist limits, and detector architectures. They do not substitute for manufacturer calibration charts.
