Background and model assumptions

Read before changing the controls
Theory primer

A radiographic exposure is a chain: the tube produces the primary beam, the patient attenuates it, the remnant beam reaches the receptor, and the receptor converts that signal into an image. In this lab, the detector response is simplified to make cause-and-effect visible.

Inverse-square law: I₂ = I₁ × (d₁ / d₂)²   ·   Magnification: M = SID / SOD   ·   OID: SID − SOD

Increasing kVp increases beam energy and penetration; increasing mAs increases photon quantity. Increasing OID increases magnification and unsharpness, while a longer SID generally reduces magnification when SOD is held constant.

Model boundary: Results are directionally and mathematically useful for learning. They are not calibrated exposure charts, dosimetry, or manufacturer specifications.
