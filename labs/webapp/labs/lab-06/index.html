Background Theory & Mathematical Principles

Geometric optics of radiographic image formation & resolution limits
Physics Primer

1. Spatial Resolution & Spatial Frequency

Spatial resolution (recorded detail or sharpness) refers to the structural sharpness of recorded anatomical boundaries and the distinct separation of adjacent microstructures. In medical imaging, spatial resolution is objectively quantified in terms of spatial frequency, expressed in line pairs per millimeter ($lp/mm$). One line pair comprises one radiopaque lead bar and one adjacent radiolucent space of identical width ($w$):

Spatial Frequency vs. Object Dimension:
$$f = \frac{1}{2 \times w} \quad [lp/mm]$$
Where $w$ is the physical width of a single bar in millimeters. For example, resolving trabecular bone microarchitecture at $5.0\text{ lp/mm}$ requires resolving objects as tiny as $w = 1 / (2 \times 5) = 0.10\text{ mm}$ ($100\ \mu\text{m}$).

2. Geometric Magnification Factor ($M$)

Because x-rays diverge isotropically from the target focal spot in a conical beam, any anatomical structure positioned at a distance from the image receptor is projected with dimensional enlargement (size distortion):

Magnification Formula:
$$M = \frac{\text{Image Size}}{\text{Object Size}} = \frac{SID}{SOD} = \frac{SID}{SID - OID}$$
Where $SID$ is Source-to-Image Distance, $OID$ is Object-to-Image Distance, and $SOD = SID - OID$ is Source-to-Object Distance. Percentage enlargement is given by $\Delta L\% = (M - 1) \times 100\%$.

3. Focal Spot Geometric Blur ($U_g$) & Penumbra Formation

The x-ray tube focal spot is not an infinitesimal point source; it possesses finite physical dimensions (effective focal spot size $F$, typically $0.3\text{ mm}$ for fine/micro detail, $0.6\text{ mm}$ for extremity bone, or $1.2\text{ mm}$ for standard broad-beam exposures). Because photons originate across the entire surface of this rectangular area, edge rays cross along object boundaries, projecting two distinct geometric zones onto the detector:

  • Umbra: The completely shielded, uniform shadow core representing the true geometric outline of the object.
  • Penumbra ($U_g$, Geometric Unsharpness): The partial shadow or blurred transition zone encircling the umbra where detector pixels receive photons from only part of the focal spot.
Geometric Unsharpness Equation:
$$U_g = F \times \frac{OID}{SOD} = F \times \frac{OID}{SID - OID} = F \times (M - 1)$$
Focal spot blur ($U_g$) is directly proportional to focal spot dimension ($F$) and $OID$, and inversely proportional to $SOD$. Minimizing $OID$ and maximizing $SID$ (e.g., $180\text{ cm}$ tele-radiography) suppresses penumbra width.

4. Limiting Spatial Resolution & Total System Unsharpness

When the width of the penumbra ($U_g$) equals or exceeds the width of a single bar in a line-pair pattern, the alternating radiopaque and radiolucent lines blend together into an indistinguishable gray blur. The theoretical limiting spatial resolution ($R_{\text{limit}}$) permitted by focal spot geometry is approximately:

Geometric Limiting Resolution:
$$R_{\text{limit}} \approx \frac{1}{2 \times U_g} \quad [lp/mm]$$
In diagnostic imaging, the overall clinical image blur ($U_t$) represents the quadrature summation of geometric blur ($U_g$), patient kinetic motion blur ($U_m = v \times t$), and image receptor unsharpness ($U_r$, detector pixel pitch / scintillator light spread): $$U_t = \sqrt{U_g^2 + U_m^2 + U_r^2}$$ Patient motion ($U_m$) is the most frequent clinical cause of non-diagnostic resolution failure, mitigated by rigid immobilization and shortest feasible exposure time ($t$).

Physical Model Boundary: Simulation telemetry demonstrates relative geometric optical ray tracing and penumbra propagation. Equipment tolerances, focal spot blooming under high mA, and detective quantum efficiency (DQE) are pedagogical approximations.

1. Focal Spot Size ($F$)

Physical dimension of the electron bombardment area on the tungsten target. Dual-focus x-ray tubes provide a small focus ($0.6\text{ mm}$) for fine bony extremity imaging and a large focus ($1.2\text{ mm}$) for thick body parts requiring high tube current ($mA$) loading.

Small: $0.6\text{ mm}$ (Sharp) | Large: $1.2\text{ mm}$ (Double Blur)

2. Object-to-Image Distance ($OID$)

Separation between anatomical structure and detector plane. Increasing $OID$ creates an air gap, rapidly elevating both magnification ($M$) and penumbra blur ($U_g$). Minimizing $OID$ by placing the part in direct contact with the receptor is the first rule of sharpness.

Contact: $OID \approx 0–2\text{ cm}$ | Air-Gap: $OID \ge 15\text{ cm}$

3. Source-to-Image Distance ($SID$)

Total distance between focal spot and image receptor. Increasing $SID$ from $100\text{ cm}$ to $180\text{ cm}$ (e.g., standard PA chest) enlarges $SOD$, straightens diverging beam rays, significantly decreases penumbra width, and reduces cardiac magnification.

Standard: $100\text{ cm}$ | Extended: $180\text{ cm}$ ($U_g$ reduced ~45%)

4. Line-Pair Resolution Phantom

A precision lead foil phantom etched with calibrated groups of alternating lead strips and radiolucent spacers calibrated in $lp/mm$. Radiographers image the phantom to verify system limiting spatial resolution during routine Quality Control (QC) evaluations.

Test Range: $0.5–5.0\text{ lp/mm}$ ($w = 1.0\text{ mm}$ down to $0.1\text{ mm}$)

Experimental Protocol & Scientific Method

Controlled-variable investigation of focal spot blur and geometric magnification
Controlled Protocol

Standardized Experimental Aims:

  1. Aim 1 (Focal Spot Effect): Keep $SID = 100\text{ cm}$ and $OID = 5\text{ cm}$ constant. Vary focal spot from $0.3\text{ mm} \to 1.0\text{ mm} \to 2.0\text{ mm}$. Observe how $U_g$ increases in direct linear proportion while magnification ($M$) remains strictly fixed.
  2. Aim 2 (OID Air-Gap Effect): Keep focal spot $F = 1.0\text{ mm}$ and $SID = 100\text{ cm}$ constant. Increase $OID$ from $0\text{ cm} \to 5\text{ cm} \to 20\text{ cm}$. Observe the rapid rise in magnification factor ($M$) and focal spot blur ($U_g$), causing loss of high-frequency line-pair visibility.
  3. Aim 3 (SID Restoration Effect): At an elevated $OID = 15\text{ cm}$, increase $SID$ from $100\text{ cm}$ to $180\text{ cm}$. Verify that enlarging $SOD$ significantly restores edge definition by reducing $U_g$ and bringing $M$ closer to unity.
  4. Aim 4 (Spatial Frequency Cutoff): Set test pattern frequency to $4.0\text{ lp/mm}$ and determine the exact focal spot and OID combinations where pattern visibility drops below diagnostic threshold ($<75\%$).
