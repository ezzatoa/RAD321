1. Learning objectives

  1. Explain how kVp and mAs alter the primary beam and receptor exposure.
  2. Use SID, SOD, and OID to calculate magnification and predict geometric change.
  3. Apply the inverse-square law to compare beam intensity at different distances.
  4. Explain how patient attenuation changes the remnant beam and visible image intensity.
  5. Record a reproducible virtual exposure and interpret its results.

2. Background concepts

A radiographic exposure is a chain: the tube produces the primary beam, the patient attenuates it, the remnant beam reaches the receptor, and the receptor converts that signal into an image. In this lab, the detector response is simplified to make cause-and-effect visible.

Inverse-square law: I₂ = I₁ × (d₁ / d₂)²   ·   Magnification: M = SID / SOD   ·   OID: SID − SOD

Increasing kVp increases beam energy and penetration; increasing mAs increases photon quantity. Increasing OID increases magnification and unsharpness, while a longer SID generally reduces magnification when SOD is held constant.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Write a prediction about increasing SID while all other controls remain fixed.
  2. Record a baseline run.
  3. Change only SID and record a second run.
  4. Return SID to baseline, change only OID, and record a third run.
  5. Use the run log to compare intensity and magnification.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

A portable AP chest image is enlarged because the detector could not be placed close to the patient. Which adjustment best limits magnification if clinically practical?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Compare two exposure runs using kVp and mAs. What changed in the primary beam and why?
  • 2. Show one inverse-square calculation using values from your run.
  • 3. Explain how OID affected magnification and recorded detail.
  • 4. Critical thinking: identify one patient-dependent and one radiographer-controllable cause of a weak remnant beam.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
