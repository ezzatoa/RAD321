1. Learning objectives

  1. Describe the difference between CR and DR image recording.
  2. Distinguish direct and indirect digital conversion pathways.
  3. Relate pixel pitch, matrix size, and field of view to sampling resolution.
  4. Use a virtual test pattern to identify resolution limits.
  5. Select a digital receptor strategy for a clinical task.

2. Background concepts

CR uses a photostimulable phosphor plate that is read by a laser; DR uses a flat-panel detector that converts the exposure into electronic data. Indirect DR uses a scintillator to convert X-rays to light before charge collection; direct DR converts X-rays to charge.

Sampling frequency: approximately 1 / (2 × pixel pitch) in cycles per unit distance (Nyquist limit). Smaller pixels can support higher sampling resolution, but detector construction, focal spot blur, motion, and dose also matter.

Matrix size and field of view work together: for a fixed field, a larger matrix means smaller pixels. Resolution is not guaranteed by a high matrix alone if the rest of the imaging chain is limiting.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict how reducing pixel pitch changes sampling resolution.
  2. Record a baseline receptor setup.
  3. Change only pixel pitch or matrix and record a second run.
  4. Switch CR/DR pathway and record a third run.
  5. Compare matrix-derived pitch, effective pitch, and effective resolution.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

A department needs immediate bedside images without cassette scanning. Which receptor best fits the workflow?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Compare CR and DR in terms of recording, readout, and workflow.
  • 2. Calculate the pixel size for a 35 cm field and 2048-pixel matrix.
  • 3. Explain why sampling resolution is not identical to total image sharpness.
  • 4. Critical thinking: describe one reason to choose CR and one reason to choose DR.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
