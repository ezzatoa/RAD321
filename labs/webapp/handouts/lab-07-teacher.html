1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Sharpness & Detail II — Distortion.
  2. Explain that the visual is a teaching model; numerical trends and formulas matter more than decorative appearance.
  3. Demonstrate one baseline run without revealing the required comparison.
  4. Require one-variable-at-a-time changes and references to run numbers in written answers.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Predict the image shape when the anatomy is tilted relative to the IR.
  2. Record an aligned baseline.
  3. Change only the part angle and record a second run.
  4. Change CR angle to test a projection-specific correction and record a third run.
  5. Compare shape factor and distortion index.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: A long bone appears foreshortened because it was not aligned with the receptor. What is the first correction?

Best response: Restore the required anatomy-IR-CR alignment

Foreshortening is an alignment problem; exposure quantity and display processing do not correct shape.

5. Knowledge-check key

The best general method for minimizing unintended shape distortion is to:
Align the anatomy and IR correctly and use the required CR relationship — Distortion is primarily a geometry/alignment problem; exposure quantity does not correct shape.
Unintended shape distortion is minimized by:
Correct anatomy, IR, and CR alignment — Shape representation depends on alignment geometry.
Foreshortening means the object appears:
Shorter — Foreshortening represents the object as shorter than its actual projected length.
A sharp image can still be unacceptable if it is:
Distorted — Sharpness does not guarantee correct shape or diagnostic positioning.

6. Grading and final-exam preparation

  • Simulation credit requires 3 unique settings, not a completion click.
  • Give calculation/interpretation credit only when the student cites the run evidence.
  • Use the clinical response and critical-thinking worksheet item as practical-final preparation.
  • The self-rubric is advisory; the instructor assigns the official score from the exported evidence.
