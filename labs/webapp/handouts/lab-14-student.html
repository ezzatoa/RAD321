1. Learning objectives

  1. Apply Stage E (Evaluate) of the IMAGE framework as a structured, 5-domain radiographic critique.
  2. Verify anatomical coverage: distal femur, femorotibial joint space, proximal tibia/fibula, and 4-sided collimation margins.
  3. Assess positioning geometry: true AP condylar symmetry, open joint space, and ⅓ proximal fibular head superimposition.
  4. Evaluate exposure physics: Exposure Index (EI), Deviation Index (DI), quantum mottle, and trabecular sharpness.
  5. Apply ALARA repeat decision rules: distinguish fatal non-diagnostic defects from rectifiable administrative/display variances.

2. Background concepts

The IMAGE Process Stage E (Evaluate) provides the final, rigorous quality assurance check before a radiograph is transmitted to PACS for radiologist interpretation. Clinical image evaluation follows 5 structured pillars:

  • 1. Anatomical Coverage & Collimation: Ensure the entire Volume of Interest (VOI) is included (distal ⅓ femur to proximal ⅓ tib/fib) without clipping critical landmarks, with clear 4-sided collimation margins to limit scatter.
  • 2. Positioning & Alignment: For a True AP Knee, the femoral epicondyles must be parallel to the IR, the femorotibial joint space fully open, the patella centered over the distal femur, and exactly ⅓ of the proximal fibular head superimposed behind the lateral tibial condyle.
  • 3. Exposure & Noise Physics: Verify detector exposure using the standard Exposure Index (Target EI = 200, DI = 0.0). Avoid photon starvation (DI < −1.0, severe quantum mottle) and unnecessary dose creep (DI > +1.0).
  • 4. Identification & Lead Markers: Verify patient demographics (2 unique identifiers) and the presence of a physical radiopaque lead side marker ('R' or 'L') placed in the primary beam outside the VOI.
  • 5. Artifacts & Motion: Eliminate preventable external radiopaque items (zippers, snaps, jewelry) and assess for patient motion blur or hardware detector line defects.
ALARA Repeat Rule: A repeat exposure is clinically justified only when a defect obliterates diagnostic efficacy and cannot be rectified without re-irradiating the patient.
Missing Marker Policy: A missing lead marker on an otherwise diagnostic image MUST NOT be repeated. Departmental policy dictates electronic annotation and dual-technologist verification.
Critique Weighting: Score = 0.30·SAnat + 0.25·SPos + 0.25·SExp + 0.10·SMark + 0.10·SArt.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict whether a missing side marker alone justifies a repeat exposure under ALARA.
  2. Record a Gold Standard diagnostic AP Knee baseline.
  3. Select a severe rotation or collimation clipping defect and record a second run.
  4. Select a missing marker or quantum mottle case and record a third run.
  5. Compare critique scores, ALARA repeat verdicts, and actionable corrective directives.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

An AP Knee radiograph demonstrates pristine anatomy, true AP alignment, open joint spaces, and optimal exposure (EI 200), but the radiographer forgot to place the physical lead side marker. What is the correct clinical action under ALARA?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, and ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Complete the 5-domain IMAGE critique checklist for your baseline run and state your clinical disposition.
  • 2. Analyze the effect of +18° external rotation on the fibular head, patella, and joint space visibility.
  • 3. Compare a fatal diagnostic failure (e.g. quantum mottle / clipped anatomy) with a non-fatal administrative defect (missing marker) under ALARA.
  • 4. Critical thinking: formulate a precise corrective action instruction for a junior radiographer presenting an underexposed, rotated radiograph.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
