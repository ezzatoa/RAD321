2. Background concepts

Image noise represents random, unwanted variation in pixel values that degrades image clarity and limits the visibility of low-contrast anatomical structures and pathological lesions. In digital radiography, total noise consists of two independent components:

  • Quantum Noise (Quantum Mottle, σq): Caused by the random, statistical arrival of discrete X-ray photons at the image receptor (Poisson process). At low detector exposure (low mAs or low DQE), fewer photons form the image, causing large relative fluctuations. Quantum standard deviation scales as σq = S / √Ndetected.
  • Intrinsic Electronic Noise (σint): A fixed background noise floor generated by detector readout electronics, thermal fluctuations, and analog-to-digital conversion, independent of radiation dose.
  • Total Combined Noise (σtot): Because quantum and electronic noise are statistically independent, they combine in quadrature: σtot = √(σq2 + σint2).
Object Signal (S, in arbitrary units `a.u.`): The mean transmitted X-ray intensity / digital pixel value in a region of interest. Expressed in arbitrary units (a.u.) to represent relative detector signal levels.
Detected Quanta (N): N ∝ mAs × DQE. Total number of photons detected and converted.
Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR): SNR = S⁄σtot = (Mean Signal S) / (Total Noise σtot). Measures overall signal prominence relative to noise.
Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR): CNR = |Starget − Sbackground|⁄σtot. Measures how easily a lesion can be distinguished from surrounding tissue.
Rose Criterion: A visual psychophysics standard stating that an SNR ≥ 5.0 (or CNR ≥ 3.0) is required for an observer to reliably detect a subtle object.
Detective Quantum Efficiency (DQE): DQE = SNRout2 / SNRin2. Measures how efficiently a detector converts incident radiation into image signal without adding excessive noise.

Why does increasing Object Signal increase Noise SD? In Poisson statistics, absolute variance scales directly with the signal amplitude (σ ∝ √N). Therefore, a higher baseline transmission produces higher standard deviation on a fixed display scale. However, increasing total beam intensity (mAs) delivers more total photons, improving relative SNR (SNR ∝ √mAs).

Simulation outputs use educational relative values (arbitrary units, a.u.) to demonstrate fundamental statistical laws without requiring equipment-specific dosimetric calibration.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict how a fourfold mAs increase changes quantum SNR.
  2. Record a low-mAs baseline (e.g., 2 mAs, DQE 0.7).
  3. Increase only mAs (e.g., 10 mAs, then 40 mAs) and record runs to verify SNR ∝ √mAs.
  4. Restore mAs, adjust DQE or intrinsic noise, and record runs to identify whether quantum or intrinsic noise dominates.
  5. Assess whether the central nodule target meets the Rose Criterion (SNR ≥ 5).

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.
