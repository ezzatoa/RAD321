1. Learning objectives

  1. Recall all five stages of the IMAGE process.
  2. Perform initial patient and system setup decisions.
  3. Select manual exposure factors appropriate to a virtual patient.
  4. Prepare and position anatomy while considering comfort and artifacts.
  5. Guide the beam, collimate, place markers, and give patient instructions.

2. Background concepts

The IMAGE mnemonic organizes radiographic recording and analysis: Initial patient/system setup, Manual selection of exposure factors, Anatomy positioning and preparation, Guide the beam and patient, and Evaluate the image and recording process. Labs 13–14 divide the process into recording and evaluation.

In this lab, you will make decisions for a virtual AP knee examination. Each decision has a clinical consequence. A correct exposure factor cannot rescue poor positioning, missing anatomy, absent markers, or unnecessary collimation.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict which IMAGE recording stage is most affected by poor collimation.
  2. Record a baseline AP-knee setup.
  3. Change exposure factors and record a second run.
  4. Change SID or collimation for a third run.
  5. Use the readiness domains to identify the weakest stage.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

The patient reports pain and cannot fully extend the knee. What should happen before exposure?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Record your choices at I, M, A, and G and explain one consequence of an incorrect choice.
  • 2. Why should patient identification and IR selection happen before exposure-factor selection?
  • 3. List three preparation or positioning actions that reduce repeats.
  • 4. Critical thinking: explain how collimation can support both image quality and radiation protection.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
