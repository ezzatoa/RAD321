1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Digital Image Recording.
  2. Explain that the visual is a teaching model; numerical trends and formulas matter more than decorative appearance.
  3. Demonstrate one baseline run without revealing the required comparison.
  4. Require one-variable-at-a-time changes and references to run numbers in written answers.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Predict how reducing pixel pitch changes sampling resolution.
  2. Record a baseline receptor setup.
  3. Change only pixel pitch or matrix and record a second run.
  4. Switch CR/DR pathway and record a third run.
  5. Compare matrix-derived pitch, effective pitch, and effective resolution.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: A department needs immediate bedside images without cassette scanning. Which receptor best fits the workflow?

Best response: Wireless DR flat panel

A wireless DR panel provides direct electronic readout without a separate cassette scanner.

5. Knowledge-check key

For a fixed field of view, increasing matrix size generally:
Makes pixels smaller and increases sampling frequency — A larger matrix over the same field produces smaller pixels and a higher sampling frequency, although other limits remain.
CR uses:
A photostimulable phosphor plate and laser reader — CR stores the image on a PSP plate that is scanned by a laser.
For fixed FOV, increasing matrix size makes pixels:
Smaller — More samples across the same field produce smaller matrix-derived pixels.
The Nyquist frequency is approximately:
1/(2 × pixel pitch) — Nyquist sampling limit equals one over twice the sampling interval.

6. Grading and final-exam preparation

  • Simulation credit requires 3 unique settings, not a completion click.
  • Give calculation/interpretation credit only when the student cites the run evidence.
  • Use the clinical response and critical-thinking worksheet item as practical-final preparation.
  • The self-rubric is advisory; the instructor assigns the official score from the exported evidence.
