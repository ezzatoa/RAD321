The Imaging Chain & the Radiographer's Role
Week 1 of 15 Session Length: 2 hours Format: Individual, computer-based
Purpose of this document: Use this as your quick-reference guide at the start of the lab session — a 10–15 minute briefing outline, timing plan, key talking points, common student pitfalls, and an answer key for the worksheet/knowledge-check questions.

1. Session Opening Script (~10 minutes)

  1. Introduce the virtual lab format: "Today's lab is an interactive digital simulation. It lets you explore the imaging chain concepts hands-on, at your own pace, and as many times as you need — you'll achieve all 7 learning objectives just as you would in a traditional lab session."
  2. Direct students to open labs/lab-01/index.html (double-click the file, or open it in any browser) and fill in their Student Information fields immediately (name, ID, section) — this is required for their eventual submission report.
  3. Briefly preview the 4 stations and worksheet so students know the session's structure and time budget.
  4. Remind students that their work autosaves locally in the browser, and to use "Export JSON Backup" if they need to continue on a different device.

3. Key Talking Points Per Station

  • Station 1 (Tube): Emphasize the electron path: cathode → vacuum gap → anode. Ask students "why does the anode rotate?" before they click the hotspot (answer: heat distribution — reinforces later noise/heat-loading discussions).
  • Station 2 (4 Stages): This ordering activity is foundational — every later lab (contrast, sharpness, noise, artifacts) maps back to one of these four stages. Consider pausing here to preview how Lab 4 (density) relates to Stage 1/3, and Lab 8 (scatter) relates to Stage 2.
  • Station 3 (Receptors): Clarify that CR and DR are both "digital" but differ in mechanism (cassette-based/laser-read vs. direct panel readout) — a common point of confusion.
  • Station 4 (RT Role): Reinforce that "recording" happens before exposure/processing and "analysis" happens after — a useful distinction that recurs throughout the IMAGE mnemonic in Labs 13–14.

4. Common Student Pitfalls

Confusing SOD and OID: Some students think OID is the same as SID. Clarify using the diagram: SID = full path (tube→receptor); SOD = tube→patient; OID = patient→receptor. SID = SOD + OID (approximately, for typical setups).
Thinking "digital" means "no darkroom" only: Students sometimes forget CR still uses a physical cassette (just no wet chemistry) — it's a middle ground between film and DR.
Underexposure critical-thinking question: Weaker answers only cite "not enough mAs." Push students to also consider patient thickness (Stage 2) and receptor sensitivity (Stage 3) as valid root causes.

5. Answer Key — Knowledge Check & Worksheet

Q (Station 1): Function of the collimator?
Restricts/shapes the useful beam size reaching the patient — reduces dose and scatter.
Q (Station 2): Stage most affected by patient thickness/density?
Patient Interaction.
Q (Station 3): Which receptor needs wet chemical processing?
Screen-film cassette.
Q (Station 4): Judging image contrast/sharpness quality belongs to which role?
Image Analysis.
Final Q1: Inverse square law effect of distance on intensity?
Decreases with the square of the distance.
Final Q2: Definition of attenuation?
Reduction in X-ray beam intensity as it passes through matter.
Worksheet Q2 — Four stages + one factor each (sample):
1) Propagation — kVp/mAs/distance; 2) Interaction — patient thickness/density; 3) Capture — receptor sensitivity; 4) Processing — chemical development or digital algorithm.
Worksheet Q3 — Recording vs Analysis (sample):
Recording example: selecting exposure factors. Analysis example: judging if the image needs to be repeated.
Worksheet Q5 — Electron source vs photon source:
Cathode (releases electrons) and Anode (produces X-ray photons when struck by electrons).
Worksheet Q6 — Critical thinking (underexposure), acceptable stages:
Stage 1 (too little kVp/mAs, excess filtration, long SID without compensation), Stage 2 (unusually thick/dense patient causing excess attenuation), Stage 3 (low receptor sensitivity or processing error). Full credit requires at least two stages with sound reasoning.

6. Grading Notes

The rubric (20 points) is visible to students inside the lab app for self-assessment before submission. When grading the submitted report:

  • The report's "Interactive Simulation & Activity Results" section shows completion status and score (e.g., 4/4 hotspots identified) for each station — use this to verify Criterion 2 (Simulation Execution) objectively.
  • The "Knowledge Check Results" section shows first-attempt quiz accuracy — useful as a quick proxy for Criterion 1 (Conceptual Understanding), though the worksheet answers should carry more weight for depth.
  • Worksheet answers are transcribed verbatim into the report — grade Criteria 3 and 4 directly from that section.
