1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Visibility Factors II — Contrast.
  2. Explain that the visual is a teaching model; numerical trends and formulas matter more than decorative appearance.
  3. Demonstrate one baseline run without revealing the required comparison.
  4. Require one-variable-at-a-time changes and references to run numbers in written answers.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Predict how increasing kVp changes subject contrast.
  2. Record a baseline run.
  3. Increase only kVp and record a second run.
  4. Restore kVp, change receptor/display processing, and record a third run.
  5. Separate subject-contrast changes from display-contrast changes.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: A soft-tissue task requires subtle differences to remain visible. Which approach is most defensible?

Best response: Use adequate penetration, preserve signal, then optimize display contrast

Diagnostic contrast requires adequate signal first; display processing cannot recover clipped or missing information.

5. Knowledge-check key

Which change generally increases subject contrast in film-screen radiography?
Decrease kVp while maintaining adequate penetration — A lower kVp generally increases differential attenuation and subject contrast, provided penetration remains adequate.
Subject contrast originates primarily from differences in:
Tissue attenuation — Different tissues attenuate the beam differently, producing subject contrast.
Higher kVp generally produces:
Longer-scale subject contrast with greater penetration — Higher kVp generally lowers differential attenuation while increasing penetration.
Digital processing can:
Change displayed contrast of captured data — Processing remaps captured data but cannot restore missing signal or anatomy.

6. Grading and final-exam preparation

  • Simulation credit requires 3 unique settings, not a completion click.
  • Give calculation/interpretation credit only when the student cites the run evidence.
  • Use the clinical response and critical-thinking worksheet item as practical-final preparation.
  • The self-rubric is advisory; the instructor assigns the official score from the exported evidence.
