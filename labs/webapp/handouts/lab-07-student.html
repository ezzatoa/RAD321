Lab 7: Geometric Distortion Lab

Sharpness & Detail II — Distortion
Week 7 of 15Session: 2 hoursAssessed individual work
Submission evidence: at least 3 unique runs, a written prediction, completed concept and clinical challenges, 4 knowledge-check questions, worksheet responses, and the rubric.

1. Learning objectives

  1. Define size, shape, and positional distortion.
  2. Relate part lateral offset, part tilt, OID, and SID to elongation and foreshortening.
  3. Predict the effect of off-center lateral displacement and part tilt under a perpendicular beam.
  4. Use a virtual alignment check to reduce distortion.
  5. Explain why anatomy-specific positioning criteria matter.

2. Background concepts

Distortion is a misrepresentation of object size or shape. It occurs when the anatomical part is angled or displaced away from the perpendicular central ray. Foreshortening occurs when the anatomical part is tilted relative to the perpendicular beam. Elongation occurs when the part is displaced laterally into the divergent peripheral beam.

Alignment goal: anatomy centered over perpendicular CR and anatomy plane ∥ IR. Moving an object laterally away from the central ray exposes it to divergent oblique rays that physically elongate its recorded shadow.

Distortion is not simply “blur.” A sharp but distorted image may still fail the diagnostic criterion because anatomy is represented incorrectly.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict the image shape when the anatomy is tilted relative to the IR.
  2. Record an aligned baseline.
  3. Change only the part angle and record a second run.
  4. Change CR angle to test a projection-specific correction and record a third run.
  5. Compare shape factor and distortion index.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

A long bone appears foreshortened because it was not aligned with the receptor. What is the first correction?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Record one elongation and one foreshortening configuration from the simulator.
  • 2. Explain the alignment relationship among the anatomy, IR, and CR for a routine projection.
  • 3. Distinguish distortion from unsharpness.
  • 4. Critical thinking: give one reason a projection may intentionally use an angled CR.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
