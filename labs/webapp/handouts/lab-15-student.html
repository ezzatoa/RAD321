Lab 15: Radiographic Artifact Identification Lab

Image Quality & Artifacts — Root-Cause Analysis & ALARA Gating
Week 15 of 15Session: 2 hoursAssessed individual work
Submission evidence: at least 3 unique runs, a written prediction, completed concept matching and clinical scenario challenges, 4 knowledge-check questions, 4-item worksheet responses, and the 20-point rubric.

1. Learning objectives

  1. Recognize and classify radiographic artifacts across modern Digital (DR), Computed (CR), and Screen-Film modalities.
  2. Distinguish hardware, technical/exposure, patient preparation, guidance/alignment, and processing etiologies.
  3. Trace an artifact back to its origin stage in the 5-stage IMAGE process (Initial setup, Manual factors, Anatomy prep, Guidance, Evaluation).
  4. Apply ALARA repeat decision rules: distinguish fatal Volume of Interest (VOI) obstruction from recoverable post-processing variances or hardware service quarantines.
  5. Formulate immediate corrective actions and departmental preventive Quality Control (QC) protocols to eliminate recurrence.

2. Background concepts & Modality Artifact Guide

An artifact is any unwanted visual density, distortion, or defect on a radiograph that does not correspond to actual anatomical structures. Proper artifact analysis requires systematic evaluation of pattern geometry, modality physics, and clinical significance.

5-Stage IMAGE Root-Cause Tracing Framework:
• Stage I (Initial System Setup): Receptor selection, plate optical erasure, detector calibration.
• Stage M (Manual Factors & Technique): kVp, mAs (photon fluence), filtration, exposure timer.
• Stage A (Anatomy & Patient Preparation): Patient gowning, jewelry removal, immobilization, breath-hold instructions.
• Stage G (Guidance & Alignment): Central ray centering, tube-part-receptor alignment, grid selection and leveling.
• Stage E (Evaluation, Processing & Display): Automatic processor transport, laser reader optics, TFT readout electronics, PACS windowing.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Prediction: Formulate and write a hypothesis regarding whether a persistent detector line defect should trigger a patient repeat or equipment quarantine under ALARA.
  2. Run 1 (Baseline): Load Case 0 (Optimal Clean Baseline) with 0% severity and record your reference run.
  3. Run 2 (Digital / Hardware Defect): Select a hardware or digital artifact (Case 1: DR Column Line or Case 2: CR Moiré Aliasing) and evaluate VOI obstruction and root-cause stage.
  4. Run 3 (Patient Preparation or Alignment): Select Case 4 (Metal Zipper) or Case 6 (Grid Cutoff) and analyze ALARA repeat criteria versus prevention protocols.
  5. Windowing & View Modes: Test PACS window presets (Bone, Soft Tissue, High Contrast), Grayscale Inversion, and Diagnostic VOI ROI Overlay.

4. Clinical challenge

Scenario: A fixed vertical straight radiopaque line appears in the exact same detector coordinates on multiple consecutive patient examinations across different radiographers.

Action: Choose the appropriate ALARA-compliant clinical response in the simulation, evaluate the explanatory feedback, and justify why equipment quarantine is required over patient re-exposure.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Artifact Recognition: Identify three artifact cases from your experiment, describe their visual characteristics, and state their imaging modalities (DR, CR, or Film).
  • 2. Systematic IMAGE Tracing: Select one artifact and trace its root cause through the 5-stage IMAGE framework (I, M, A, G, E), explaining exactly where and why the failure occurred.
  • 3. Corrective vs. Preventive Strategies: Distinguish an immediate clinical corrective action from a departmental preventive Quality Control (QC) strategy for a digital artifact.
  • 4. ALARA Gating & Hardware Quarantine: Apply ALARA principles to justify why hardware detector line dropouts and non-obscuring peripheral artifacts MUST NOT result in routine patient re-exposures.
