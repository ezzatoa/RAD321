1. Learning objectives

  1. Define spatial resolution and spatial frequency.
  2. Calculate magnification from SID and SOD.
  3. Calculate focal-spot geometric unsharpness.
  4. Predict how focal spot size, OID, and SID affect recorded detail.
  5. Use a resolution pattern to compare two imaging geometries.

2. Background concepts

Spatial resolution is the ability to distinguish small, closely spaced structures. Spatial frequency is often expressed in line pairs per millimeter. Geometric unsharpness is blur caused by the finite focal spot and object-to-receptor separation.

M = SID/SOD · Ug = F × OID / SOD, where F is focal-spot size. Smaller focal spots, smaller OID, and larger SOD reduce geometric blur.

Magnification can reveal small structures in some applications, but it also increases blur unless geometry is controlled. The goal is not the largest image; it is adequate diagnostic detail with acceptable dose and positioning.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict which control most strongly changes geometric unsharpness.
  2. Record a baseline run.
  3. Increase only focal-spot size and record a second run.
  4. Restore focal spot, increase OID, and record a third run.
  5. Compare Ug, magnification, resolution limit, and pattern visibility.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

A fine-detail extremity image is blurred by geometry. Which combination best improves recorded detail?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Record two focal-spot/OID combinations and calculate Ug for each.
  • 2. Explain how magnification differs from blur.
  • 3. What does a line-pair pattern test, and why does spatial frequency matter?
  • 4. Critical thinking: explain a situation where deliberate magnification may be useful despite its trade-offs.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
