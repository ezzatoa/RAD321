Lab 13 Instructor Guide

The IMAGE Process, Part 1 — Recording Stages
Week 13120 minutesAssignment and practical-final preparation
Assessment integrity: This document contains answers. It is intentionally not linked from the student dashboard.

1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Image Analysis I — IMAGE Recording Stages.
  2. Explain that the visual is a teaching model; numerical trends and formulas matter more than decorative appearance.
  3. Demonstrate one baseline run without revealing the required comparison.
  4. Require one-variable-at-a-time changes and references to run numbers in written answers.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Predict which IMAGE recording stage is most affected by poor collimation.
  2. Record a baseline AP-knee setup.
  3. Change exposure factors and record a second run.
  4. Change SID or collimation for a third run.
  5. Use the readiness domains to identify the weakest stage.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: The patient reports pain and cannot fully extend the knee. What should happen before exposure?

Best response: Adapt positioning safely, support the patient, and document the limitation

Patient-centered adaptation and documentation preserve safety and reduce motion/repeats.

5. Knowledge-check key

Which action belongs to the Guide stage of IMAGE?
Centering the CR and placing the marker — Guide includes CR alignment, collimation, marker placement, breathing instructions, and completing the exposure.
The I in IMAGE represents:
Initial patient and system setup — Initial setup includes identification, receptor, SID, and system preparation.
Marker placement and CR centering belong mainly to:
Guide the beam and patient — Guide includes alignment, collimation, marker, instructions, and exposure.
Removing jewelry belongs to:
Anatomy positioning and preparation — Preparation removes external artifacts before positioning and exposure.

6. Grading and final-exam preparation

  • Simulation credit requires 3 unique settings, not a completion click.
  • Give calculation/interpretation credit only when the student cites the run evidence.
  • Use the clinical response and critical-thinking worksheet item as practical-final preparation.
  • The self-rubric is advisory; the instructor assigns the official score from the exported evidence.
