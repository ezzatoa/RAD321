1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Recording Film Radiographs.
  2. Explain that the visual is a teaching model; numerical trends and formulas matter more than decorative appearance.
  3. Demonstrate one baseline run without revealing the required comparison.
  4. Require one-variable-at-a-time changes and references to run numbers in written answers.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Predict the effect of excessive developer temperature.
  2. Record the recommended baseline condition.
  3. Change only temperature and record a second run.
  4. Restore temperature, then move the safelight closer and record a third run.
  5. Compare development activity, fog risk, and quality index.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: Several films show uniform gray fog, including unexposed control film left in the darkroom. What should be checked first?

Best response: Darkroom light leaks and safelight conditions

Fog on unexposed control film implicates darkroom light/safelight or chemical fog, not exposure geometry.

5. Knowledge-check key

A uniformly gray film with correct exposure factors is most consistent with:
Safelight or light leak fog — Uniform gray fog points to unwanted light exposure or chemical fog; inspect the darkroom and safelight first.
The latent image in film is formed mainly by changes in:
Silver halide crystals in the emulsion — Exposure creates an invisible latent-image pattern in silver halide crystals.
The fixer primarily:
Removes unexposed silver halide and hardens emulsion — Fixer removes unexposed crystals and stabilizes the visible image.
Excessive developer activity most commonly makes film:
Too dark and potentially fogged — Excessive time/temperature increases development and density/fog.

6. Grading and final-exam preparation

  • Simulation credit requires 3 unique settings, not a completion click.
  • Give calculation/interpretation credit only when the student cites the run evidence.
  • Use the clinical response and critical-thinking worksheet item as practical-final preparation.
  • The self-rubric is advisory; the instructor assigns the official score from the exported evidence.
