1. Learning objectives

  1. Define Window Width (WW) and Window Level (WL) in digital imaging and CT post-processing.
  2. Relate window width to displayed contrast (Look-Up Table slope) and window level to image brightness.
  3. Interpret a multi-peak anatomical histogram, identify active passband regions, and recognize black clipping and white saturation.
  4. Explain digital exposure latitude, automatic rescaling, and how post-processing can hide exposure creep.
  5. Calculate active display intervals and recommend clinical window presets for bone, mediastinum, and lung tasks.

2. Background concepts & formulas

Digital radiographic detectors and CT systems capture wide linear dynamic ranges. Grayscale transformation (Look-Up Table / Windowing) translates broad raw attenuation into display grays (0–255):

Active Display Passband: Tlow = WL − WW/2  to  Thigh = WL + WW/2 (HU).
Look-Up Table (LUT) Contrast Gain: G = 255 / WW (gray levels per HU).
IEC Deviation Index (DI): DI = 10 · log₁₀(EI / EIT) (Target: −1.0 to +1.0 DI).
Subtle Lesion CNR: CNR = ΔGray / σeffective, where σeffective = √(σdisplay² + σquantum² · 100/EI).

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated diagnostic workstations or equipment dosimetry logs.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict the visual and quantitative effect of narrowing window width from 400 to 120 HU.
  2. Record a standard mediastinum baseline (WW: 400, WL: 40, EI: 100).
  3. Narrow only the window width (WW: 120, WL: 45) and record a second run to evaluate lesion contrast gain and tissue clipping.
  4. Explore bone detail (WW: 1500, WL: 400) or underexposure/noise (EI: 55, DI: −2.6) for a third run.
  5. Compare active display range, contrast gain, lesion CNR, and dose indicators.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical decision case

Scenario: During a routine audit of portable chest radiographs, all images appear visually crisp with normal brightness, but 40% of cases exhibit Exposure Index (EI) values corresponding to a Deviation Index (DI) above +2.5. What is the correct professional action?

Evaluate the case in the interactive app, review the feedback, and connect your decision to ALARA principles and exposure indicator monitoring.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Record two window/level settings and describe which anatomy is emphasized.
  • 2. Calculate the display range for width 500 and level 300.
  • 3. Explain why a visually acceptable digital image can still represent excessive exposure.
  • 4. Critical thinking: recommend window settings for a high-contrast bone task versus a subtle soft-tissue task.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
