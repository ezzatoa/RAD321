1. Learning objectives

  1. Define optical density and explain why it is logarithmic.
  2. Construct and interpret a simplified H&D characteristic curve.
  3. Identify toe, straight-line, and shoulder regions.
  4. Compare film speed, latitude, and contrast using curve parameters.
  5. Use a sensitometer/densitometer simulation to support an exposure decision.

2. Background concepts

Optical density (OD) describes film blackening: OD = log₁₀(I₀ / Iₜ), where I₀ is incident light and Iₜ is transmitted light. Higher OD means less light passes through the processed film. A characteristic curve plots OD against the logarithm of exposure.

Toe: low-density region with limited separation · Straight line: useful exposure range · Shoulder: saturation region · Gamma: slope of the straight-line portion.

Film speed is the exposure needed to reach a reference density; latitude is the useful exposure range. A steeper curve has higher contrast but narrower latitude.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict how increasing gamma changes the H&D curve.
  2. Record a baseline point in the straight-line region.
  3. Move exposure into the toe and record a second run.
  4. Move exposure into the shoulder or change gamma and record a third run.
  5. Compare OD, transmitted light, region, and latitude.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

Two films require different exposures to reach the same reference OD. Film A reaches it with less exposure. Which statement is correct?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Record two OD readings and use the logarithmic definition to explain what the values mean.
  • 2. Identify the toe, straight-line, and shoulder on your curve and describe each.
  • 3. Compare the effects of increasing gamma and increasing latitude.
  • 4. Critical thinking: recommend a faster or slower film for a dose-sensitive exam and justify the trade-off.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
