1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Connect the simulation variables to Image Analysis II — IMAGE Evaluation Stage (Stage E).
  2. Explain that the PACS review monitor renders simulated anatomical positioning, quantum noise, markers, and artifacts in real time.
  3. Demonstrate how the Gated ALARA Evaluation Engine distinguishes fatal diagnostic errors (requiring repeat) from administrative/display variances (where repeats are prohibited).
  4. Emphasize that students must cite specific run numbers, EI/DI values, and rotation angles in their written responses.

3. Expected experimental trend

  1. Run 1 (Baseline): Optimal AP Knee (Anatomy 95%, Rotation 0°, 8 mAs, Lead 'R' Marker, No Artifact). Critique score ~97/100, ALARA verdict: ACCEPT & TRANSMIT TO PACS.
  2. Run 2 (Positioning Defect): +18° External Rotation. Joint space obscured, fibular head superimposed. Critique score drops to ~42/100, ALARA verdict: REJECT & REPEAT (Justified). Corrective action: Rotate 18° internally.
  3. Run 3 (Missing Marker Dilemma): Anatomy 95%, Rotation 0°, 8 mAs, Marker Missing. Critique score ~88/100, ALARA verdict: ACCEPT WITH ADMINISTRATIVE CORRECTION (Do NOT Repeat — ALARA Gate).

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: An AP Knee radiograph demonstrates pristine anatomy, true AP alignment, open joint spaces, and optimal exposure (EI 200), but the radiographer forgot to place the physical lead side marker. What is the correct clinical action under ALARA?

Best response: Follow institutional electronic annotation policy with dual-technologist verification; do NOT repeat under ALARA.

Rationale: Patient re-exposure solely to capture a physical marker violates ALARA when diagnostic efficacy is 100% intact. Departmental electronic annotation protocols must be used.

5. Knowledge-check key

Which statement best reflects a professional image critique under the IMAGE framework?
The image has adequate anatomy and exposure but lacks a physical side marker; follow local electronic annotation policy before repeating under ALARA. — A critique identifies specific evidence, determines diagnostic impact, and applies ALARA.
On an AP Knee radiograph, which anatomical relationship confirms True AP positioning without rotation?
Exactly ⅓ of the proximal fibular head is superimposed behind the lateral tibial condyle — Complete separation indicates medial rotation; excessive overlap indicates lateral rotation.
Under radiation protection regulations and ALARA principles, when is a repeat exposure justified?
Only when critical diagnostic information cannot be recovered and the diagnostic question cannot be answered — Repeat is a last resort justified solely by lost diagnostic efficacy.
An AP Knee image exhibits severe quantum mottle (EI 50, DI -6.0) obscuring fine trabeculae. What is the proper corrective action for the repeat?
Increase exposure technique from 2 mAs to 8 mAs (target EI 200) — Quantum mottle is photon starvation; raising mAs restores diagnostic photon fluence.

6. Grading and practical-final preparation

  • Ensure students have tested at least 3 distinct clinical cases in the simulator.
  • Worksheet responses must explain both why a fatal defect justifies repeat and why missing markers DO NOT justify repeat under ALARA.
  • Verify that student corrective action statements are specific and actionable (naming exact angle changes or technique adjustments).
