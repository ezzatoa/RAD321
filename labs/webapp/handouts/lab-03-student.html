2. Background concepts

Screen-film radiography uses a double-emulsion film between intensifying screens. X-rays stimulate the screens, which emit light and expose silver halide crystals. This invisible pattern is the latent image. Chemical processing converts it into a visible image through developer, fixer, washing, and drying.

Safe processing sequence: expose → developer → rinse/stop → fixer → wash → dry. The safelight must be appropriate for the film emulsion and placed far enough away to prevent fog.

Temperature, time, replenishment, and contamination control are quality variables. A film can be correctly exposed yet diagnostically poor if the darkroom or processor introduces fog, stains, or incomplete development.

Simulation outputs are educational relative values, not calibrated dose or equipment specifications.

3. Experimental procedure

  1. Predict the effect of excessive developer temperature.
  2. Record the recommended baseline condition.
  3. Change only temperature and record a second run.
  4. Restore temperature, then move the safelight closer and record a third run.
  5. Compare development activity, fog risk, and quality index.

Controlled-variable rule: after the baseline, change one variable at a time. Duplicate settings do not count as a new run.

4. Clinical challenge

Several films show uniform gray fog, including unexposed control film left in the darkroom. What should be checked first?

Choose the response in the app, read the feedback, and be prepared to justify the decision using image quality, diagnostic value, or ALARA.

5. Worksheet guidance

  • 1. Label the film/cassette layers and state the function of screens and emulsion.
  • 2. Describe how an X-ray exposure becomes a latent image and then a visible image.
  • 3. Explain what would happen if developer temperature is too high or too low.
  • 4. Critical thinking: distinguish a darkroom fog problem from an underdeveloped film using the appearance and process evidence.

Refer explicitly to run numbers and numerical results. A trend without evidence receives limited credit.
