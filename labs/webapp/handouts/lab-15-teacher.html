1. Opening briefing (10–12 minutes)

  1. Curricular Alignment: Connect simulation variables to Lecture 15 (Image Quality & Artifacts) and Bushong Chapter 10/19 (Analogue vs Digital artifacts).
  2. Dual-Panel Interface: Demonstrate the 1000×380 dual-panel workbench: left PACS viewport (with anatomical AP knee structures, DICOM tags, collimators, and WW/WL interactive tools) and right IMAGE Root-Cause / ALARA scorecard.
  3. ALARA Gating Principle: Emphasize that artifacts do NOT automatically warrant patient re-exposure. Re-irradiation is strictly prohibited if diagnostic integrity within the Volume of Interest (VOI) is intact or recoverable via post-processing. For hardware failures (e.g. DR column dead line), repeating exposures on patients is clinically negligent; the receptor must be quarantined immediately.
  4. Controlled Runs: Ensure students record at least 3 unique runs covering baseline, digital/hardware artifacts, and patient prep/alignment defects.

4. Clinical scenario key

Prompt: A fixed vertical straight radiopaque line appears in the exact same detector coordinates on multiple consecutive patient examinations across different radiographers. What is the most appropriate, ALARA-compliant first action?

Correct Answer: Remove the detector from clinical service immediately and initiate biomedical QC / service review (Quarantine)

Rationale: A persistent, coordinate-locked linear defect across different patients represents an irreversible TFT detector hardware fault (dead readout line). Repeating exposures on patients violates ALARA without solving the defect; the detector must be quarantined immediately.

5. Knowledge-check key

1. A wavy, corduroy-like zebra interference pattern (Moiré artifact) in digital/CR imaging originates from:
Spatial frequency aliasing between stationary grid strips and laser scan sampling frequency — Moiré artifact occurs when stationary grid frequency is close to the digital sampling frequency (Nyquist limit) and grid lines run parallel to the laser scan line.
2. A persistent straight line appearing in the exact same detector coordinates across multiple patient examinations indicates:
TFT detector array column hardware failure requiring service quarantine — Fixed hardware line dropouts are detector TFT readout failures. ALARA mandates quarantining the receptor rather than re-irradiating patients.
3. Under the ALARA principle, a radiographic artifact mandates an immediate patient repeat exposure ONLY when:
The artifact obscures critical diagnostic anatomy in the Volume of Interest (VOI) and cannot be resolved by digital post-processing — ALARA strictly prohibits re-exposure unless diagnostic information within the VOI is truly lost and irrecoverable by digital post-processing.
4. Dendritic branching black marks (tree static) on film radiographs are prevented primarily by:
Maintaining darkroom relative humidity between 40%–60% and gentle film handling — Static discharge occurs in dry environments (humidity <40%) due to friction when pulling film quickly from cassettes or boxes.

6. Worksheet Grading Guidance (20 points)

  • Q1 (Artifact Recognition & Modality - 4 pts): Must correctly name 3 distinct cases, identify their visual traits (e.g. geometric shape, optical density, frequency pattern), and correctly link to modality (DR, CR, Screen-Film).
  • Q2 (5-Stage IMAGE Tracing - 5 pts): Must break down one chosen artifact across the 5 stages (I: Initial, M: Manual, A: Anatomy, G: Guidance, E: Evaluation) and pinpoint the exact failure stage with physical mechanism.
  • Q3 (Correction vs Prevention - 5 pts): Must clearly contrast the single-image immediate clinical remedy (e.g. post-processing windowing, cassette re-erasure) from institutional departmental QC (e.g. weekly calibration, humidity monitoring, PM schedule).
  • Q4 (ALARA Gating Justification - 3 pts): Must explain why hardware detector failures and non-obscuring peripheral artifacts must NOT result in patient re-exposure under ALARA.
  • Completeness & Quality - 3 pts: All runs recorded with thorough observations, no empty fields.
